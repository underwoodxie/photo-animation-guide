# How to Animate Old Family Photos Without Making Them Look Fake

A grandparent's wedding portrait, a faded school photo, a snapshot from a 1970s holiday. Seeing one of these move for the first time can be surprisingly emotional. It can also go badly: rubbery faces, warping backgrounds, eyes that blink the wrong way. Here's how to keep it on the right side of that line.

## 1. Restore before you animate

Animation amplifies flaws. Scratches flicker, dust spots crawl, and blur turns into smearing. Spend five minutes first:

- Scan at 600 DPI or higher, or photograph the print flat under even daylight.
- Crop out the album border and any glare.
- Fix big tears and spots before moving on.

## 2. Ask for less motion, not more

The most convincing animations are subtle: a slow head turn, a soft smile, a breath, hair moving slightly. Big gestures on an old portrait almost always break the face. If your tool takes a text prompt, describe small, calm movements.

## 3. Keep clips short

Three to five seconds is plenty. Longer clips give the model more time to drift away from the original face.

## 4. Generate a few versions

Results vary between runs. Make three or four and keep the one where the person still looks like *themselves*. Show it to a relative who knew them; they'll spot an off result instantly.

## 5. Try it

You can test all of this in the browser with [AI Photo Animator](https://aiphotoanimator.ai/): upload a photo, describe the motion, and get a short video back. Start with a clear, front-facing portrait for the best first result.

## A note on respect

These are real people, and many of them can't be asked anymore. Share animated photos with the family first, and label them as AI-animated when you post them publicly.
